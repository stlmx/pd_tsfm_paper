# EPSR (Electric Power Systems Research, ISSN 0378-7796) — PD recognition, circuit-breaker mechanical diagnosis, and few-shot / transfer / foundation-model / LLM work (2021–2026)

Method note (applies to every entry below): venue, DOI, volume, article number, author list and cover date for every paper listed were verified against the Crossref REST API (`api.crossref.org/journals/0378-7796/works` and `api.crossref.org/works/<DOI>`). Every DOI has prefix `10.1016/j.epsr`. ScienceDirect article pages, the Insights page and the Elsevier article API all returned HTTP 403 or "Invalid API Key" to this researcher. Elsevier does not deposit abstracts to Crossref, Semantic Scholar holds no abstracts for these papers, and OpenAlex was rate-limited. So method details come only from (i) search-engine abstract snippets, (ii) university research portals (XJTU Pure), and (iii) one full-text PDF from a repository (PolyU IRA). Where none of these covered a paper, its details are marked **not found**. Citation counts are Semantic Scholar values as of 2026-10-09.

Volume and date convention: EPSR publishes about one volume per month. Crossref cover dates are vol 241 = Apr 2025, vol 248 = Nov 2025, vol 250 = Jan 2026, vol 254 = May 2026, vol 261 = Dec 2026, vol 264 = Mar 2027, vol 265 = Apr 2027. Papers with DOIs `j.epsr.2026.*` in vols 262–265 are articles in press that are online in 2026 but carry 2027 cover dates. They fall within the 2021–2026 window by online year. Cite them as "Electric Power Systems Research, vol. 265, Art. no. 1142xx, 2027 (in press, online 2026)" and recheck the final cover date before submission.

## Q1. Which EPSR papers address PD recognition (GIS, cable, transformer, switchgear; UHF/HFCT/IEC 60270; PRPD/PRPS/pulse-waveform inputs)? Title, authors, year, vol/art., DOI, input, model, dataset, split, baselines, accuracy

### Takeaway
EPSR published at least 20 ML-based PD papers between 2022 and 2026, with a sharp increase in 2025–2026. Recent work has moved from feature engineering plus classifiers toward label-scarce and open-set settings, including contrastive learning, prototype learning, semi-supervised learning, few-shot learning and federated learning. The XJTU groups (Jing Yan / Yanxin Wang / Jianhua Wang, and Guan-Jun Zhang) and B. Vigneshwaran's group (Basharan) publish repeatedly on PD in EPSR. **No EPSR PD paper found uses a pretrained time-series foundation model.** That is the white space the planned paper would fill. Per-paper dataset sizes, splits and accuracies are mostly paywalled and were **not found** except where noted.

### Inferences
- The EPSR PD ML papers cover all four target equipment classes: transformer (109241, 111860, 113485, 114260, 114020, 114283), GIS (111993, 112685, 108162, 113307), cable (114197, 113910, 112155, 109838, 111601) and insulator/tree (109287). Inputs are mostly PRPD/PRPS images, time–frequency transforms (wavelet, TQWT, multi-synchrosqueezing, MFCC) or pulse-sequence features. None of the abstracts seen mentions a pretrained time-series backbone.
- The two closest technical neighbours to a "phase-anchored adaptation of TSFMs for few-shot PD" paper are FEPCL (112685: label scarcity, Fourier amplitude/**phase**, prototypes, open-set) and CSEST (111993: explicit argument that ViT patching **breaks PRPD phase continuity**). The planned paper should position against both. CSEST's phase-continuity argument directly motivates phase anchoring. Zheng et al. 2023 (109241) is the canonical EPSR "few-shot PD" citation that reviewers will expect.
- The specimen-level split in 111601 (separate cables for train and test) gives the planned paper an EPSR precedent for splitting by specimen or recording session rather than randomly by sample.

## Q2. Which EPSR papers address high-voltage circuit-breaker / switchgear mechanical condition diagnosis?

### Takeaway
EPSR has a small but real line of HVCB mechanical-diagnosis papers. They include Ye et al. 2022 (attention capsule CNN on raw vibration, small-sample, 94.7%), a few-shot-flavoured 1D-CNN + prototypical network for the energy-storage mechanism (2026), a limited-sample GIS disconnector paper (2026), and physics or model-based spring-fatigue and clearance-joint papers. The same XJTU group (Jing Yan / Yanxin Wang) writes both the CB and the GIS-PD papers.

## Q4. Typical structure of an EPSR equipment-diagnosis paper; 3–5 best structural templates and why

### Takeaway
The one EPSR PD + deep-learning paper whose full text could be read (Shamsoddini et al. 2025, 111601) is 12 two-column pages, about 10.5k words including references, with 15 figures, 9 tables and 35 references. It follows the pattern Introduction → Theoretical background → Methodology → Results and discussion (test model, Case A, Case B, comparison with other methods, practical implementation considerations) → Conclusion → CRediT / Competing interests / Acknowledgments / Data availability. Submission to acceptance took about 2 months, from received 31 Dec 2024 to accepted 5 Mar 2025. The recommended structural templates are 111601, 109241, 112685, 111993 and 114258, with 108003 as a CB alternative.

### Cited Findings
- **Full-text structure of 111601** (EPSR 245, 2025; published version from the PolyU repository) — [PolyU PDF](https://ira.lib.polyu.edu.hk/bitstream/10397/117782/1/1-s2.0-S0378779625001932-main.pdf):
  - Front matter: ARTICLE INFO with keywords (Attenuation, Cable, Deep learning, Partial discharge, Transfer function, Traveling wave) and an unstructured abstract of one paragraph. Dates printed: "Received 31 December 2024; Received in revised form 26 February 2025; Accepted 5 March 2025; Available online 17 March 2025".
  - Headings: 1 Introduction; 2 Theoretical background (2.1 Cable model; 2.2 Cable-specific attenuation characteristic; 2.3 Analysis of cable-specific attenuation characteristic); 3 Methodology (3.1 Two-level signal segmentation, with 3.1.1 Correlation-based signal partitioning and 3.1.2 Incident and reflected PD pulses identification; 3.2 Improved arrival time recognition; 3.3 Fault distance calculation); 4 Results and discussion (4.1 Establishment of the test model; 4.2 Performance evaluation under Case A conditions; 4.3 Performance evaluation under Case B conditions; 4.4 Single-end algorithms comparison; 4.5 Practical implementation considerations and capabilities of the proposed method); 5 Conclusion; CRediT authorship contribution statement; Declaration of competing interest; Acknowledgments; Data availability; References.
  - Counts: 12 pages, 15 figures, 9 tables, 35 references. The network-architecture table lists trainable parameters (11,777). A dataset table gives cable parameters and sizes (10,920 train+val / 3,280 test). Train and test sets come from **different cables**. The paper adds noise (white noise with SD 5 mV, periodic noise) at controlled SNR, reports hardware (RTX 3050) and has a dedicated "practical implementation" subsection covering sampling rate and runtime.
- **Zheng Q. et al. 2023 (109241)** — the template for few-shot + PRPD/PRPS + real-time positioning, and the most-cited EPSR few-shot PD paper (99 citations). It is free to read (bronze OA) — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S037877962300130X)
- **Wang Y. et al. 2026 (112685)** — the template for a label-scarce + open-set + noise-robust contribution list (three-module design, five keywords, a single headline accuracy ">95%" for two tasks) — [XJTU portal](https://scholar.xjtu.edu.cn/zh/publications/fourier-enhanced-prototype-contrastive-learning-for-partial-disch/)
- **Liu Y. et al. 2025 (111993)** — the template for arguing from PRPD phase structure (that ViT patching breaks phase continuity). Its evaluation visuals are raincloud plots and t-SNE — [XJTU portal](https://scholar.xjtu.edu.cn/en/publications/a-swin-transformer-enhanced-depthwise-convolution-with-dynamic-ch/)
- **Zheng X. et al. (114258)** — the only EPSR paper found about adapting large time-series models. Its title follows a "Making X work for Y" adaptation pattern. It is hybrid OA, so its full text should be readable from ScienceDirect with a browser — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.epsr.2026.114258)
- **Ye X. et al. 2022 (108003)** — a compact small-sample template (raw-signal 1D CNN, attention plus capsule, a single headline small-sample accuracy of 94.7%, noise-robustness test) — [XJTU portal](https://scholar.xjtu.edu.cn/en/publications/a-novel-capsule-convolutional-neural-network-with-attention-mecha/)

### Inferences
- **Recommended templates (ranked):**
  1. **111601 (Shamsoddini et al. 2025)** for **overall section architecture and experimental reporting**. It is verified in full text and OA. It reports a split by physical specimen, an explicit dataset table, a parameter count, a noise and SNR protocol, a "practical implementation considerations" subsection and a Data availability statement. All of these map directly onto a real-lab-PD-data paper.
  2. **109241 (Zheng Q. et al. 2023)** for **problem framing**: few-shot PD with PRPD/PRPS inputs, a real-time or lightweight claim, transformer PD.
  3. **112685 (Wang Y. et al. 2026)** for **contribution framing in the label-scarce/open-set setting**, and as the strongest in-journal competitor. Fourier amplitude/phase features sit close to phase anchoring.
  4. **111993 (Liu Y. et al. 2025)** for **the phase-continuity motivation paragraph and visualisation style** (t-SNE, raincloud plots).
  5. **114258 (Zheng X. et al., in press)** for **how EPSR frames adapting a large pretrained TS model**. Read its full text (OA) to copy its ablation and baseline structure.
- For the planned paper's Results section, a defensible EPSR layout would be: experimental platform and specimens; dataset table (specimens/classes/recordings, split by specimen or session); main few-shot results (N-way K-shot); comparison with CNN/LSTM/Swin/contrastive baselines and other TSFMs; ablation of phase anchoring; noise or cross-specimen robustness; practical-implementation subsection (latency, parameters, hardware). This is an inference built from the 111601 structure and the abstracts above.

## Q5. Journal scope signals, Journal Insights stats (time to first decision, review time, acceptance rate), APC / OA options

### Takeaway
EPSR is a hybrid Elsevier journal (subscription with an optional gold-OA APC) that publishes about one volume per month. Elsevier's two pages give **conflicting** turnaround and APC figures: USD 2,870 vs USD 3,050, and 72 vs 117 days to decision after review. No official acceptance rate was found. One scope signal stands out: EPSR asks for contributions "directly related to the influence on power system operation rather than presenting the issues of particular network elements only." This is a risk for a pure PD-classification paper. Even so, EPSR clearly keeps publishing equipment-PD ML papers.

### Cited Findings
- The Insights page shows: APC **USD 2,870** (excluding taxes, may be reduced at submission); about **1 day to first decision**; **72 days** to a decision after review; **148 days** to acceptance; **10 days** from acceptance to online publication — [EPSR Insights (via search snippet)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/journal/electric-power-systems-research/about/insights)
- The journal homepage, which appears to be more recent, shows: APC **USD 3,050**; **46 days** to first decision; **117 days** to decision after review; **10 days** from acceptance to online publication — [EPSR homepage (via search snippet)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/journal/electric-power-systems-research). **These two sources conflict.** Both pages returned 403 to direct fetch, so the figures could not be checked against the live pages.
- A third-party site lists an "indicative APC" of USD 2,480, but it does not report an acceptance rate — [journalmetrics.org](https://www.journalmetrics.org/journal/electric-power-systems-research)
- Scope wording on the EPSR homepage: submissions "should be directly related to the influence on power system operation rather than presenting the issues of particular network elements only". Papers mainly about electricity prices or market economics, or isolated components without demonstrated technical impact on the wider system, are out of scope. **Review articles are by invitation only** — [EPSR homepage (via search snippet)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/journal/electric-power-systems-research)
- An EPSR editor's 2019 presentation describes the focus as "the electric power system from a systems point of view" — [PowerTech 2019 slides](https://attend.ieee.org/powertech-2019/wp-content/uploads/sites/107/2019/07/p3-3.pdf)
- OA options seen on the papers above: hybrid gold OA under CC BY-NC 4.0 (112155, 111601) and CC BY 4.0 (114120), per Crossref licence metadata — [Crossref 10.1016/j.epsr.2025.111601](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1016/j.epsr.2025.111601). Green OA through institutional repositories was also seen, e.g. PolyU hosts the published PDF of 111601 — [PolyU IRA](https://ira.lib.polyu.edu.hk/handle/10397/117782). Some articles are free to read without an OA licence (bronze; 109241, per Unpaywall).
- Observed turnaround for one PD + DL paper (111601): submission to acceptance in **64 days**, with a single "revised form" date (i.e. one revision), and acceptance to online in **12 days** — [PolyU PDF](https://ira.lib.polyu.edu.hk/bitstream/10397/117782/1/1-s2.0-S0378779625001932-main.pdf)
- Publication cadence derived from Crossref cover dates: vol 241 Apr 2025 → vol 254 May 2026 → vol 265 Apr 2027, i.e. one volume per month — [Crossref EPSR works](https://api.crossref.org/journals/0378-7796/works?query.bibliographic=partial+discharge&filter=from-pub-date:2021-01-01&rows=200)

### Inferences
- To defuse the "particular network elements only" scope clause, the planned paper's Introduction and Conclusion should state the system-level impact explicitly: PD-driven insulation failure risk for transformers, GIS and cable outages; condition-based maintenance; deployability across substations with few labelled defects. The many EPSR equipment-PD papers since 2022 suggest this clause is not applied strictly to diagnosis papers, but the framing should still be included.
- The "1 day to first decision" figure probably reflects fast desk decisions, i.e. a high desk-rejection rate. That is an inference; no acceptance rate is published.

- Official acceptance rate, Impact Factor and CiteScore: **not found** (Insights page inaccessible).
- The date stamp on each set of Insights figures is unknown, so it cannot be said which of the conflicting turnaround figures is current.
